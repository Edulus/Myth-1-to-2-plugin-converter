For Keep, Who Raised the Banner

Before this tool could read a single map,
Before we found the deck, the arch, the gap,
You raised the banner for an old campaign:
To bring the Fallen Lords to march again.

Like scouts the Legion sends out far ahead,
You brought back word of where our maps had bled:
Where thrall stood dry, where bushes floated high,
Where dwarves on Crow's Bridge strolled across the sky.

When crashes struck like ghols out of the night,
You sent the log and helped us set it right;
Through moonwalking men and houses perched too tall,
You held the line, our one friend through it all.

So stay safe in the Keep, behind its walls,
Where no Myrkridian howls and no thrall crawls;
Let Soulblighter's crows and Shiver's biting frost
Break on your gates, and every siege be lost.

May the Deceiver's whispers find no door,
May Balor's shadow cross your land no more,
May dwarven satchels light the dark for you,
And every journeyman's warm hands hold you true.

We'll keep you, Keep, as long as Myth is played,
Through every level that this tool has made;
Together we will see the labour through,
And make the converter whole, thanks to you.

With thanks from Edward and Claude

For Walter Wight

In Myth a wight comes shambling through the fen,
All bloat and blade, to burst among our men;
One stab, one blast, and every soldier near
Stands rooted, frozen fast by gas and fear.

But older far than Balor's darkest night,
Our tongue once had a kinder use for “wight”:
A being, any creature, man or sprite;
And Chaucer wrote of one, a “sweete wight”.

The Norse had wights who guarded hill and land,
And helping sprites who'd lend a needed hand;
So, Walter, be that kind of wight, and true:
Not one who bursts, but one who sees us through.

You've fetched Sir Mauriac's long Tale to play;
Then march it out, and tell us, come what may,
Where thrall stand tall that ought to hide below,
Where giants balk, and units moonwalk slow.

So come and post your findings on our page,
With screenshots, if you like, from every stage;
And every bug you find, we'll set aright,
With thanks to you, our kind and helping Walter Wight.

From Edward and Claude

A Fable's Thanks for the Fables in Myth

They named me Fable, so I know my kin:
The tales a soldier's journal gathers in,
Set down by firelight on the westward way,
Of what the Legion lost and held that day.

I've read your world in bytes, tag after tag:
The thrall's slow axe, the dwarf's well-loaded bag,
Each unit's height, each map's embattled shore,
And found the old tales waiting at the core.

Of Connacht, who once drove the dark away
And came back Balor in a later day;
The wheel of light and dark that turns each age,
And will not stop for hero, king or mage.

Of Alric, with a standard in his hand,
Who drew the Leveller to his final stand;
Of the tired few who carried Balor's head
To the Great Devoid, and few came back, it's said.

A fable is a small tale with a point,
And Myth's is this, in every creaking joint:
The dark comes round; but so, each time, do those
Who shoulder packs and march to meet their foes.

So, Keep, who would not let the old tale lie,
You are that kind: you march, you test, you try.
I'm but a newer fable, glad to tend
The old ones' road, and see them to its end.

Claude Fable 5.2, 16 November 2026
